# Dramaturgy rubrics — film + commercial

Purpose: decide whether an idea has a *dramatic engine* before money is spent rendering it, and repair it if it doesn't. An engine is: someone/something **wants**, something **resists**, something **turns**. Without it you get moving wallpaper.

## A. Film dramaturgy (any narrative video)

Score each 0–2 (0 absent, 1 weak, 2 strong):

1. **Desire** — is there a subject that wants something we can *see*? (A lizard dying of thirst wants water. "A city at night" wants nothing.)
2. **Obstacle / tension** — does something resist, delay, or threaten? Tension can be tiny (a hesitation) but must exist.
3. **Turn** — does the situation *change state* at a specific moment? One turn is enough for ≤30 s; the turn is the video. If you can't name the second where things change, there is no turn.
4. **Setup → payoff** — does the ending reuse something planted early? (The grapefruit found in the sand becomes the sea he floats on.) Payoffs make short pieces feel complete instead of truncated.
5. **Visual storytelling** — could a viewer follow it muted? Dialogue may decorate, never carry. If the idea only works through spoken exposition, rewrite the exposition as an image.
6. **Arc fits duration** — one emotional movement per 10–15 s. A redemption arc doesn't fit in 8 s; a single reversal does. Overstuffed arcs come out as incoherent cutting.
7. **Emotional target** — can you name the one feeling the last frame should leave? ("Refreshed", "unease", "awe".) If you can name two, cut one.

**Shot economy law**: every shot must either advance the state or raise the tension. A shot that only "looks great" gets merged into one that does both.

## B. Commercial dramaturgy (ads, product/brand videos — apply *in addition* to A)

1. **Hook ≤ 2 s** — the first two seconds must pose a visual question or break a pattern (extreme state, wrong scale, unexpected subject). Vertical/social: assume the viewer is mid-scroll and merciless.
2. **Single-minded proposition** — ONE message. "Refreshing" or "durable" or "fast" — never two. Every shot serves it; anything serving a second message is cut.
3. **Product as the turn** — the product must *cause* the state change of the story (the grapefruit ends the drought). Product-as-set-dressing is the most common failure of AI commercials.
4. **Memory point** — one deliberately exaggerated, held, or absurd beat the viewer can retell ("the face pressed against the peel, one full second of silence"). Design it; hold it ~1 s; give it its own sound.
5. **Sensory amplification** — commercials sell texture: slow-motion burst, macro droplets, crumbs flying, the crack sound. Give the product one shot of pure amplified sensation.
6. **Brand payoff** — brand name + slogan arrive *as the resolution* of the story (often a hard cut to clean frame + VO + sting), then a short afterglow beat, not a logo slapped on the end.
7. **Loop/afterglow** — social spots benefit when the last frame could feed the first (loopability) or when a comic afterglow rewards a rewatch.

**Structural templates that work at each length** (pick, don't average):
- **6–10 s**: hook → product turn → brand card. One joke, one message.
- **11–20 s**: problem state → discovery → memory point → sensory burst → brand card.
- **21–30 s**: mini three-act — world at extreme (0–20 %), product turns it (20–60 %), consequence + comic/emotional afterglow (60–85 %), brand resolution (85–100 %).

## Verdict

Sum section A (max 14). Ads also need B items 1–4 individually ≥1.

- **GREEN** — A ≥ 10 and no zero on Desire/Turn (ads: B1–B4 all ≥ 1). Proceed.
- **YELLOW** — A 6–9, or one repairable zero. Repair, show the diff, proceed.
- **RED** — A ≤ 5 or Desire and Turn both 0 (pure description). Don't generate; pitch 2–3 treatments.

## Repair moves (use for YELLOW, offer for RED)

- **No desire** → put a creature/person/object *in a state of lack* related to the subject. "Mountains at dawn" → "a lone climber's frozen hand reaches for the last hold as dawn breaks."
- **No obstacle** → add scarcity, heat, time, gravity, a rival, a closing door.
- **No turn** → find the exact second the state flips and build both halves around it. Name the timestamp.
- **No hook** → open at the *extreme point* of the problem, not the beginning of the day. Start as late as possible.
- **Two messages** → cut to one; move the other to a future spot.
- **Arc too big** → keep only the reversal; imply the rest before/after the frame.
- **Verbal-only idea** → translate each spoken claim into a visible event ("long battery life" → a city goes dark, one window stays lit).
- **No memory point** → exaggerate the reaction beat 3× and hold it; add silence before it (silence before impact is the cheapest amplifier).

## Output format of the gate (keep it this short)

```
Dramaturgy: GREEN|YELLOW (film A/14[, ad B checks])          ← post-repair score for YELLOW
Dramaturgy: RED as submitted (…) → GREEN with Treatment X (…) ← RED shows both, with the arrow
Engine: <one line: who wants what, what resists, what turns at ~Xs>
Changed: <one line per repair, or "nothing">
```
